# The Journey Map — Working Draft v1
### The emotional journey, the four spaces, and every existing document through the one-question test.
*This is paper, not pages. Nothing gets built from it until Tim has torn it apart.*

---

## PART ONE — The journey mapped to the spaces

The journey: **alone → found my people → found Jesus again → a new way to live → help others find home.**

**Alone → arrival.** The person lands carrying loneliness, not a query. What meets them is the founding document — not a homepage *about* the project, the document itself, or its opening movement. The first screen's only job is recognition and exhale. No navigation pitch, no feature tour. *Space: the front door — which is the founding document.*

**Found my people.** Before anyone will sit at a table, they need evidence that tables exist and hold people like them. This is why the Commons cannot come last in the build order even though it's the newest idea — it delivers the second sentence ("I thought I was the only one"), and the second sentence is half the promise. *Space: the Commons.*

**Found Jesus again.** The practice itself: a table, a meal, a story, four questions, an act. *Space: the Table — the Canon of Fifty and the table pattern.*

**A new way to live.** Weeks become a year; practice becomes formation; some people want to go deeper into the why. *Spaces: the Table (the year-one rhythm) and the Library (TWG, for those who choose it).*

**Help others find home.** The person who came home sets a table for someone else — or brings the model into their church. *Space: the Workshop.*

The structural consequence: **the journey is the navigation.** Not audience doors, not a resources menu. Each stage of the journey is a door, and the four spaces sit behind them.

## PART TWO — Every document through the one-question test

Format: document → the question its reader carries → verdict.

**Founding document v4** → *"Is there a home for someone like me?"* → **KEEP.** The front door. One document, all readers.

**Canon of Fifty (+ five arc files)** → *"What story do we tell this week, and how?"* → **KEEP UNCHANGED.** The most naturally universal asset in the set. Same document whether the table meets in a kitchen or a church basement. Lives in the Table.

**Starter guide (door-one version)** → *"How do I host a table?"* → **REWRITE, ONCE, UNIVERSALLY.** The question is identical for the churched and unchurched host — the old two-doors split made this document church-inflected for no reason the reader cares about. New opening answers the emotional question first (*you're about to give someone a home*), logistics second. Church-specific hosting wrinkles become a short section, not a separate document.

**Kit-6 (hosts, year one, shepherding)** → *"How do I keep a table alive and healthy for a year?"* → **MERGE INTO UNIVERSAL HOST MATERIAL.** Same logic: a table's year-one health doesn't depend on affiliation. This likely fuses with the rewritten starter guide into one host document with two altitudes: launching, then shepherding. That's still one question — *how do I host well* — at two time horizons. Tim's call whether it's one document or two.

**Kit-4 (household & safeguarding)** → *"How do we do this safely, especially with kids?"* → **KEEP, MAKE UNIVERSAL.** Currently church-kit framed; the question is universal and the content barely changes. Safeguarding is also the one place where the no-gates principle bends to reality: it stays free and open, but it's non-optional in every host pathway.

**Kit-1 (pastor's letter)** → *"Should my church try this?"* → **KEEP, REWRITE OPENING.** Legitimate separate question, legitimate separate document. But it currently opens with logistics-anxiety relief; it should open with the pastor as a spiritually homeless person — he reads the founding document first, then this. Workshop, church wing.

**Kit-2 (posture guides)** → *"Which way does this fit my church?"* → **KEEP.** Genuine church question, no universal twin. Language sweep only.

**Kit-3 (start/integration/money/90 days)** → *"What does adopting this actually involve?"* → **KEEP.** Same treatment: sweep, and the money section rewrites to reflect the firewall (coaching now lives outside, linked not sold).

**Kit-5 (leadership team guide)** → *"How do I get my board to yes — honestly?"* → **KEEP.** Distinct question, well-scoped. Sweep only.

**Churches pitch document** → *"Why should I care?"* → **KILL AS A SEPARATE DOCUMENT.** Its question is now answered by the founding document plus the pastor's letter. A pitch document is also the wrong genre for a project that doesn't recruit — the seams show. Anything worth saving folds into kit-1.

**The Whole Gospel** → *"I want to go deeper — what was Jesus actually announcing?"* → **KEEP, PLACE.** The Library's anchor. Untouched by this pass except placement and one rule: no path requires it.

**The former brand's 3.0 manual, its 2.0 manual, and Next Build Instructions v2** → questions nobody carries anymore → **ARCHIVE OFF-PROJECT.** Historical value to Tim, corruption risk to sessions.

**Net count: roughly twenty documents become about ten.** One front door, one Canon (plus arcs), one host document (possibly two altitudes), one safeguarding document, four church-wing documents, one Library anchor, plus governance (decisions doc + amendment).

## PART THREE — The Commons, defined

**What it is:** the room that proves you're not the only one. Stories from real tables — short, first-person, unpolished. Possibly a simple count or map of tables that have said "we exist." Nothing else at first.

**What it is not:** a forum, a social network, a comment section, a community platform. Those require accounts, moderation, and maintenance that violate both the no-forms principle and the founder-light principle. The Commons is a *witness wall*, not a chat room.

**The tension that needs Tim's ruling:** the project has *no registration anywhere* locked — but a Commons requires some way for a table to say "we're here" and share a story. The lightest honest mechanism: a single email address (or equally light channel), stories curated by hand, published without last names. No accounts, no forms in the joining sense — contribution is opt-in and one-directional. Whether even that passes Tim's bar is his call, but a Commons with zero inbound channel is a museum, and the second sentence dies in a museum.

**Cold-start problem, named honestly:** at launch there are no tables and no stories. The Commons opens with the founder's own table's stories and says so plainly — anything else is staging, and staging fails the honesty section of the founding document.
