# Decisions Addendum — August 18, 2026
### Records the Review Packet rulings. Reads under the Decisions Amendment, which reads under the Founding Document. Where anything here conflicts with the founding document, the founding document wins.

## 1. The bench: split, two layers (Q1, Flag 5)

The bench is not one thing. It is:

- **First bench — local.** Every host builds their own short list in month one, before anything is wrong: a counselor or two someone at the table can vouch for, the local crisis line (988 in the US, or your country's crisis line), and any trusted pastor or elder the table has a relationship with. Local is always first, because crises are local.
- **Second bench — the team.** Behind every local list stands a small team of real people, reachable by any host, for the moments the local list isn't enough or the host doesn't know where to start. The founder serves on this team. He is never the team — founder-light governs: the architecture assumes from day one that he's gone.
- **The Workshop layer — hosting help, not crisis care.** An AI assistant, trained on the project's materials, lives in the Workshop and answers hosting questions at any hour: story prep, table dynamics, launch questions, what-do-I-do-when. **Crises never terminate in the AI.** Anything involving safety, harm, abuse, or a person in real trouble routes to humans — the local bench, the team, professionals, or emergency services. This line does not move.

## 2. The minors floor: a strong statement, not an apparatus (Q2)

Tables meet in kitchens, coffee shops, and parks. The project will not pretend to screen, register, or inspect them — there is no policing a living room, and building the theater of one would be dishonest. What the project does instead: teach these rules plainly and state them as non-optional. Two adults, nothing behind closed doors, no private digital contact between an adult and a minor, believe disclosures, report to authorities — which is the law, not our policy. Words that protect kids, not words that protect us.

The kit-4 template child-safety policy (church wing) still carries its flag: expert review by someone with real child-protection credentials before any church relies on it. That flag survives every rewrite until it's satisfied.
